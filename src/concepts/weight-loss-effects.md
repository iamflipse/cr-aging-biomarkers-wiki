# Weight-Loss Effects on the Index

## Weight change as predictor of the index (model 2)

- **Each 1 kg of weight loss** was associated with a **+0.22** (95% CI 0.09 to 0.34) increase in the quintile sum score of change — study-specific estimates all > 0 (Supp. Fig 1b).
- Larger weight decreases → larger improvements in the index, **particularly for CRP, IL-6, insulin, TNF-R1**; GDF-15 and CysC were heterogeneous.

## CR-driven weight loss

| Measure | CR group | No-CR group |
|---|---|---|
| Mean weight change | **−7.9 kg** | −1.2 kg |
| Mean BMI change | **−2.81 kg/m²** | −0.41 kg/m² |
| Group difference in weight loss | **6.7 kg** (95% CI 5.9 to 7.5) | — |

- Per-trial detail is in [Table 2](../sources/geroscience-2026-cr-aging-fulltext.md): CR arms achieved substantially greater weight loss than controls across all seven trials.

**Interpretation:** weight loss itself is a **downstream consequence** of CR and an independent predictor of index improvement — the two exposures are strongly correlated, which is why each partially accounts for the same variance in index change (see [Weight Mediation](weight-mediation.md)).
